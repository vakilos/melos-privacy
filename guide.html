Melos – User guide

Melos keeps your song guides and setlists for live gigs: lyrics, notes, scores, audio, and the setlists you play them in.

Songs

  • The Songs tab lists all your songs. Search at the top, or use the A–Z strip on the right. Each song card is two slim rows beside a small picture tile: the title with the key at its right end, and under it the artist, the tempo (a number next to a metronome) and the rhythm, with small tags at the right end for what the song has: audio, a score (PDF or image, shown as a little sheet of music), lyrics and a YouTube link.
  • Artist suggestions: when you type a song's artist, the artists you already have that match show as chips under the field (with how many songs each has); tap one to take it. The best match also shows greyed after what you typed; with a keyboard attached, Tab (or →) takes it. Case, accents and a leading "The" don't matter, so "beat" finds The Beatles.
  • Sort: the sort button next to the search box lists songs by Name (A–Z) or by Date added (newest first). Melos remembers your choice. The A–Z strip on the right is only there when sorting by name. A song you import counts as added on the day you import it.
  • Filter by artist: under the search box, a row of chips lists the artists in your library with a song count (up to six artists). With more artists it becomes a single Artists button that opens a searchable list. Tap an artist to see only their songs; clear it with the chip again, the ✕ next to it, or "All artists". The filter stays while you open songs and come back; it resets when the app is closed. It works together with the search, and the same chips are in the "Add songs" panel when you build a setlist. Names are compared ignoring capitals, extra spaces and a leading "The", so "The Beatles" and "beatles" count as one artist.
  • Tap the vinyl button to add a song. A new song has only its title, artist, key, tempo (BPM) and rhythm; everything else is added when you need it. Don't know the tempo? Tap the Tap tempo pad on the beat: after the second tap it shows the tempo and fills in the BPM field (the last few taps are averaged, and a pause of two seconds starts a new count).
  • Adding to a song: the + button in the top bar of a song's page gives Lyrics, Notes, Import a file…, Scan a page… (the camera; the pages become one PDF), Audio and YouTube link. A song has one lyrics block and one notes block, so each leaves the menu once it exists. Importing or scanning first asks what kind of file it is: Score (file), Lyrics (file), Notes (file), Chart (file), Tab (file) or Other… with a name of your own (a text file always goes with the notes). The kind decides where it shows in Live mode and how the Live mode view menu names it; tap the label on a file's row to change it later. A new lyrics or notes block opens straight in its editor; one left empty is removed again when you go back.
  • Editing: tap what you want to change. Tapping the artist/key/tempo/rhythm rows edits the song's title, artist, key, tempo, rhythm and YouTube link; tapping a lyrics or notes block opens just that block, with its subtitle and the full toolbar; the Live mode view list sits right on the page: Notes and Lyrics (if written) and one row per kind of file imported, such as Score (file). Tap a row to tick it (it is saved at once), and long-press a ticked row and drag it to put the rows in order. Long-press a lyrics or notes block to remove it (it asks first). On long lyrics the little arrow folds and unfolds the text. A new song starts with its title; the + in its top bar saves it and carries on with what you choose, and a plain Save opens its page.
  • How a song's page is ordered: the artist, then the key, tempo and rhythm (plain rows, no boxes), then the lyrics and notes blocks (in the order you chose when there are two blocks: the arrows button at the top, drag the handles, Done), and after them always the same: one Files block with everything imported, the Live mode view list, the YouTube link button and the Audio player. Live mode shows the lyrics and notes in the same order.
  • Files: audio, PDFs and images, text notes and scans.
  • What a file is: every PDF or image has a label on its row: Score, Lyrics, Notes, Chart, Tab or Other (with a name you choose, like "Horn part"). New files start as Score; tap the label to change it. The label decides where the file shows in Live mode: Lyrics files in the Lyrics view, Notes files in the Notes view, everything else in the Score view.
  • Lyrics page (Melos Pro): tap the PDF button on the first lyrics block to turn the song's lyrics, every lyrics block with its title and chords, into a page. Choose its shape: Phone screen (the default: large text that fills the screen, about the size of the Live text view) or A4 sheet (prints well, but small on a phone). It is added to the files labelled Lyrics, and you can zoom it, invert it and write on it with the pen, marker or text boxes, just like a score. The lyrics text stays as it is. If you later change the lyrics, the key, the title or other details, a page you have not written on is simply made again when you save the song (with Melos Pro). A page with your marks on it is left alone and says it is out of date: Update page makes it again from the song as it is now, in the same shape, and, because they would no longer line up, removes the marks on the old page (it asks first). A page that already exists stays usable without Pro.
  • Chords in notes: write a chord in square brackets, like [Am], [F#m7b5] or [D/F#], and it is shown as a highlighted chord (teal, without the brackets) in the song screen and in Live mode. While you type, the chord is coloured in the editor. The piano button in the notes toolbar inserts a pair of brackets for you (or wraps the selected text). Only real chords are recognised: [Verse] or [Em7x] stay plain text, and chords you write without brackets are never touched. If something that looks like a chord should stay plain text (say, a note that really is [A]), put the cursor on it and tap the crossed-out piano button: a backslash is added in front (and not shown); tap again to bring the highlight back.
  • Chords in lyrics: put chords in square brackets inside the lyrics, exactly where they change: [G]Amazing [G7]grace. In the song screen and in Live mode they are drawn above the right syllable. In the song editor, the Notes and Lyrics boxes share the same tools: formatting, song sections with rig cues, the piano button (inserts [] at the cursor, or switches a wrongly recognised chord off and on) and a row of chips: the chords already used in the song and the chords of its key to tap, or, as soon as you type [F, the chords that complete it (F, F7, Fm7, Fmaj7...). Section words such as Verse 1 or Chorus at the start of a lyrics line turn into labels too. Formatted lyrics are shown with their sections, rig cues and styles in the song screen and Live mode, with the chords still above the words. Sections in the lyrics look like a songbook rather than like the notes: a small heading in spaced capitals (VERSE 1, CHORUS) with a thin line to the edge and the rig cue beside it, a clear gap between sections, and the CHORUS heading in a solid coloured pill so the chorus is easy to find (its lines look like any other); text written on the label's line (such as x2) is shown small beside the heading; in Live mode the pedal button shows or hides the rig cues of the lyrics as well as the notes. On a lyrics page or PDF chord sheet, the sections look the same way (heading, rig cue, the chorus heading in a solid pill); other styles (bold, colours, sizes) are not printed yet. Lyrics without brackets are shown as before. Greek lyrics work like any other. If you type a chord on a Greek keyboard by mistake, Melos still understands it: the Greek letters that look like Latin ones (Α, Β, Ε, Μ and μ for m) are read as A, B, E, M and m.
  • Bar lines between chords: right after a chord, a | chip leads the chord chips: it puts a bar line, written [|], at the cursor ([Am] [|] [G] [|] [F]). It sits in the chord row, a little larger than the chords; moving to another key leaves it alone. Pasting into the lyrics always pastes plain text: fonts and colours copied from a web page stay behind.
  • Changing the key moves the chords (Melos Pro): when you change a song's key from one key to another in its details (G to A, Am to Cm) and its lyrics or notes have chords, Melos asks whether to move the chords too (Move chords, Only the key or Cancel). Setting a key for the first time, or changing between a major key and its relative minor (C to Am), only renames the key. Scores in the notes keep their notes.
  • Make chords: chord sheets copied from the web write chords as plain words, often on the line above the words. Select them and tap the magic-wand button in the text toolbar, or Make chords in the menu that appears over a selection (next to Cut, Copy and Paste). With part of a line selected, the chords in it get brackets (play Am then G becomes play [Am] then [G]). With whole lines selected (or everything), a line of chords above a line of words is moved into the words, each chord where it stood: D G over Oh Shenandoah becomes [D]Oh Shenand[G]oah; chords past the end of the words, and marks like x2, go after them; other lines of chords, and chords after a section word (Intro D G D), get brackets. Lines of words are never changed. Undo brings it all back.
  • Collapsing notes: on the song screen, the arrow on a Notes card folds it to its first line and the number of lines, to get long notes out of the way; tap it again to open them. (Lyrics cards already show three lines until opened.)
  • Transposing in the editor (Melos Pro): the arrows button in the text toolbar rewrites every chord in all the lyrics and notes blocks, and the key, by the number of semitones you choose (the dialog shows the new key). Sharps or flats follow the new key.
  • PDF chord sheet: the PDF button at the top of a song makes an A4 page with the lyrics and each chord exactly above its syllable (long lines wrap together with their chords). You can move the chords to another key just for the PDF, switch the chords off, and add the notes. For a setlist, the share button has a "Make a PDF chord sheet instead" link: one page per song, in setlist order. The PDF goes to the usual share sheet.
  • Notes can be formatted (bold, italic, sizes, colours, headings), split into sections such as Intro, Verse or Solo, and can contain rig cues for your pedals.
  • Rig cues: write a pedal setting in curly braces anywhere in the notes or the lyrics, the way chords go in square brackets: Verse {Clean + reverb}, {HX 3B}. It is drawn small, in grey monospace inside a thin box, so it reads as a setting and not as words. The { } button in the editor puts braces at the cursor (or round the selected words); while you type inside braces, the cues you used before are offered. With the cursor in a cue, the same button turns it into plain text and back (a backslash, \{like this}, keeps braces as text). The pedal button in the editor and in Live mode shows or hides every rig cue, on the song page and in Live mode too; Melos remembers it. The lyrics page prints them as "▸ Clean + reverb" (or leaves them out with rig cues switched off). Cues kept on a section label by earlier versions move into the text, right after the label, the next time you edit that block. The Melos Companion writes and shows them the same way (from version 0.1.31).
  • Pictures and sketches in notes: the picture button at the start of the editor's second toolbar row adds, on a line of its own, a Picture… from the phone (a photo of a chart, a screenshot of a riff) or a Sketch… you draw yourself. A new picture opens the crop screen first: drag the corners or edges of the frame, or the whole frame (Done or Cancel both keep the picture; nothing is cut off the file, so you can crop again later). The sketchpad has the pen, marker, highlighter and eraser in the pen colours, three widths and undo; pick its shape (wide, 4:3, square, tall) before you start. For music, choose the Staff background: staves are drawn across the sketch, and the Note tool puts a note head wherever you tap, on the nearest line or space, with ledger lines above or below the staff when it needs them (draw stems, beams or a clef with the pen). What you don't draw on stays see-through, and black ink shows white on the dark theme. Tap a picture or sketch in the editor to select it: a frame shows with a round handle in its bottom-right corner; drag the handle to make it wider or narrower (its shape stays; it snaps to Small, Medium, Large and Full width on the way). Tap the selected one again for its menu: the width (Small, Medium, Large, Full width, or the slider), crop it again or draw on it again, or remove it. Tapping the text lets go of it. They show on the song page, in Live mode and in the PDF chord sheet (with notes), travel in shared files and sync with the notes, and the Melos Companion shows and edits them too.
  • Scores in notes (Melos Pro): for a short melody line, the same button offers Score…. The score starts with the song's key and time signature (change them, and the clef, at the top). Pick a duration at the bottom (whole to sixteenth, dotted, or a rest) and tap the staff to the right of the notes: the note goes at the end, at the height you tapped, with the key's sharps or flats. When a note doesn't fit what is left of the bar, rests fill the bar and the note starts the next one; bar lines, stems, flags, ledger lines and accidentals are drawn for you, and long melodies wrap onto more lines. Tap a note to select it, then change its duration, give it a ♯, ♭ or ♮, move it a step up or down (or tap higher or lower on its column), or delete it; the bin with nothing selected removes the last note. Making a note shorter leaves rests for the time it gave up; making it longer takes the time from the notes after it in the same bar (a duration that would cross the bar line is greyed out). Play plays the melody with the instrument you pick (piano, guitar, violin, bass or clarinet) at the tempo shown (the song's to start with), highlighting each note as it sounds; undo and redo are at the top. In the notes a score keeps the same staff size, so a short melody stays short; tap it for its width or Edit score…. Everyone can see scores (on the song page, in Live mode, in the PDF, in the Companion); writing and editing them needs Melos Pro. Music symbols: the Bravura font by Steinberg, SIL Open Font License.
  • Highlights and comments (notes and lyrics): select words and tap the marker button to highlight them in yellow, green, pink, blue or orange (No highlight takes it off). The speech-bubble button puts a short comment on the selected words, or on the word at the cursor, such as "breathe" or "hold": in the editor the words are underlined and the comment shows above the field; in the song screen and Live mode it appears small and in italics right after the words ("✎ hold"), and stays with them when the text wraps or the chords are transposed. Tap the button again with the cursor in those words to change or remove the comment. Lyrics pages and PDF chord sheets do not show highlights and comments yet.
  • Repeats (notes): for sections played more than once, select from the first to the last of them (or just put the cursor in one section), tap the repeat button in the editor's top row and choose ×2, ×3, ×4 or another number. A curly bracket with the count appears beside those sections, in the editor, on the song page and in Live mode. The selection grows to whole sections. With the cursor inside a repeat the same button changes the count or removes it. Repeats made in the Melos Companion show the same way.
  • Sections write themselves: start a line with Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Theme, Solo, Interlude or Outro (any capitals, with or without a number, like verse 2, or a capital letter, like Theme A or ThemeB) and end the word with a space, a colon or a line break: it turns into the section label, spelled properly, with no need for the section menu. Only a word at the very start of a line counts, and "Introduction" is left alone. Solo labels are red, Theme and Bridge purple, the others in the app's colour. If a label is not wanted, remove it with Remove section style in the section menu, or with the clear formatting button (the crossed-out T at the end of the second toolbar row), which takes every style off the selection, or off the cursor's line when nothing is selected, section labels included; a removed label stays plain until you type the word again. Chords in [brackets] are shown in a rounded highlight.
  • Sections inside a line and arrows (notes): a section word that isn't at the start of a line becomes a small label too, without indenting anything: on an indented line (Chorus), or after an arrow or a separator (Intro → Verse → Chorus, x2 | Solo). Words in a sentence ("softer in the chorus") stay plain. Typing -> or --> gives an arrow → or ⟶, <- or <-- gives ← or ⟵; the arrows are drawn bold in the accent colour. Both show the same way in the Melos Companion.
  • Scores: open a score to zoom, turn pages and write on it with a pen, marker or highlighter, or add text boxes. Your marks stay on the score.
  • Audio: a song can have several audio files; starting one pauses the others. Recordings from phone and voice-memo apps work: MP3, M4A and AAC, WAV, FLAC, Ogg and Opus (WhatsApp and Telegram voice notes), AMR and 3GP.
  • Marks on audio: mark the places in a track you want to find again (Verse, Solo, the bridge…). + ⚑ adds one where the playhead is; to add one at a time you know, type it in the small box next to it (0:45, 1:02:30) and press Done. Each mark shows its name above the player's bar on a line that ends just above the bar, with a faint tick on the bar itself; a new mark asks for its name straight away; after that, tap the name to jump there and long-press it to rename or delete it. Bridge to the next mark (in that dialog), or a double tap between two marks (which then asks for its name), turns them into a part, drawn as a band just above the bar with its own name; tap a part to jump to its start, rename it or Unbridge it (the marks stay). Deleting a mark keeps the parts that start or end at it. To practise a part over and over, long-press it, or press ⟳ Loop under the player (it loops the part the playhead is in); while it loops the button reads ⟳ Looping …, and pressing it (or long-pressing the part again) stops. Marks go along when songs are shared, backed up, synced or sent to the Melos Companion, where they can be edited too.
  • Rename or remove a file: long-press a file (score, scan, note or audio track) and choose Rename or Remove file. Renaming only changes the name shown in Melos.
  • To delete a song, long-press it and confirm.

Setlists

  • The Setlists tab lists your setlists, grouped into projects (for example a band or a tour). Projects can be renamed and reordered.
  • Tap the crate button to create a setlist. Open Add songs to pick from your library. Drag the bar up or down to show more or fewer songs, and drag it all the way down to close the library (or tap the bar). Tap + on a song to add it: the song bounces into the list, glows for a moment and the list scrolls to it. Drag songs to change their order.
  • Pictures for projects (Melos Pro): long-press a project (or, on a setlist's page, tap the picture at the top) and choose Change picture…. Pick a photo, move and pinch it inside the square frame, then tap Use this picture. The picture replaces the generated artwork on the project, on its setlists and on the setlist page. Remove picture brings the generated artwork back. Pictures are small (256 px), go along in shared .setlist files (a project that already has a picture of its own keeps it) and in Drive sync, and follow the project when you rename it. Choosing a picture needs Pro; pictures that are already there stay visible without it.
  • Search setlists: the search box at the top of the Setlists tab finds setlists by their name or by the name of their project (folded projects open while you search). All search boxes are slim, so more of the list stays visible. Searches ignore accents and capitals, also in Greek: "αγαπη", "ΑΓΑΠΗ" and "αγάπη" all find "Αγάπη", and a final σ or ς makes no difference.
  • Use as template: to build a setlist like one you have, long-press it (or tap the copy icon next to ✎ on its page) and choose Use as template. The New setlist dialog opens with the same project, no date and the name followed by "(copy)", already selected so you can type the new one. Create makes it with the same songs in the same order and opens it; Cancel makes nothing.
  • Setlist dates: give a setlist the gig's date in its edit dialog (✎, Date). The list shows it under the name (24 OCT 2026); in each project, setlists without a date come first, then the dated ones by date. Today's and tomorrow's gigs say TODAY and TOMORROW. Once the date has passed the setlist is archived: it folds away under Archived (n) at the bottom of its project (tap to open; faded); long-press it and choose Restore to bring it back, or Archive to fold it away again. A name with a date written in it (24/10/26) gets a button to use that as the date. Dates go along with sync, shared files and the Melos Companion.
  • A song already in the setlist: adding it again asks first ("It is number 2. Add it again?"), since playing a song twice is sometimes meant (a reprise).
  • Another setlist's songs: in Add songs, the button next to the search lists your other setlists. Choose one to add its songs at the end, in the same order. If some are already here, choose Only the new ones or Add all.
  • Selecting songs: long-press a song in a setlist and choose Select, then tap more songs (or Select all). At the top: Add to setlist… copies them to another setlist or to a New setlist (handy for a short version of a set); the palette gives them a Colour; the bin takes them out of this setlist (they stay in your library). ✕ or Back stops selecting.
  • Colours: long-press a song and choose Colour (or colour several at once while selecting) to tint its row, for example to see the sets or the slow songs at a glance. The colour shows only in that setlist's list, not in Live mode or in other setlists. Colours go along with sync, shared files and the Melos Companion (which shows them).
  • Song groups: songs that belong together (the opening set, a medley, the encore) can be one group. Select them (long-press a song, Select, tap the others) and tap the Group button (three dots) at the top; give the group a name if you like and a colour. A thin frame goes round the group's songs, with the group's name, its colour and the number of songs in its top edge, and the group's songs get a ring in that colour around their number. Press and hold the name line, then drag, to move the whole group, its songs in their order. Drag a song between a group's songs (or just under its name) to put it in the group, or out of it to take it out. Tap the name line (or its ⋮) for the group's menu: Name and colour…, Select, Copy group to setlist… and Move group to setlist… (to the end of another setlist or a new one, as a group there too) and Ungroup. In Live mode the counter shows the group and the place in it, such as "Opening 2/4". Groups are free, and go along with sync, shared files and the Melos Companion.
  • Long-press a setlist to delete it.

Shows

  • The Shows tab keeps your gigs: when, where, who plays, the setlist, the fee and your notes. It reminds you of what's coming and keeps every gig you've played.
  • Tap the tickets button to add a show: Name, Artist (one of your projects, or a new one), Date, Call time (soundcheck) and On stage time, Status (Confirmed, Tentative, Cancelled), the Place (name, address, a Google Maps link), the Setlist, the Fee with its currency (the one you used last; euros for your first show) and whether it's Paid, and Notes. Nothing is saved until Save. While you type a place's name, the places of your earlier shows that match show as chips (with how many shows were there); tap one to take it, and its address and Maps link fill in too where still empty.
  • The next show sits on top in a large card (TODAY, TOMORROW, IN 5 DAYS) with Directions and its setlist. Below it the upcoming shows are grouped by Week, Month or 3 mo (three months); the week view shows weeks with Nothing booked, so gaps are easy to see. Past shows fold away by year, with a count of the ones not paid yet.
  • A show's setlist takes the show's date: change the date on the show and the setlist follows (its own edit dialog then shows the date as "from the show"). A setlist belongs to one show; for another gig with the same songs, choose From a template… in the editor, or New setlist for an empty one. The setlist's page has a link back to its show, and the show's page has Live to play the setlist.
  • A place from Google Maps: in Google Maps, tap Share on a place and choose Melos. Make a New show here, or set it as the place of an upcoming show. You can also paste a Maps link into the show's editor.
  • On a show's page: Directions and the map button (opens the place in Maps), Live to play the setlist, Mark paid, and at the top Add to calendar (your calendar app opens with the show filled in), ✎ to edit and the bin to delete. Long-press a part of the page (the times, the place, the setlist, the pay, the notes) to edit just that part. Long-press a show in the list to mark it paid or delete it; deleting a show keeps its setlist.
  • The list shows no fees, since it's often on screen at a venue: the amounts are on each show's page. A past show not paid yet says unpaid.
  • Setlists that already have a date: the first time you open Shows, Melos offers to make shows of them.
  • Stats (Melos Pro): the Stats button on the Shows tab shows a year (‹ › to change it): how many shows, what you earned (paid fees) and what you're owed (played and not paid yet), shows per month (played solid, coming dashed), per artist, the top places and every year side by side. Cancelled shows don't count, and money is added up per currency. Export as CSV sends the year's shows as a spreadsheet file (date, times, name, artist, place, status, fee, currency, paid, setlist). Without Pro the Stats screen shows its layout without the numbers.
  • Rehearsals: a show's page has two tabs, Show and Rehearsals. Tap New rehearsal to plan one: the Date, From and Until, the Place (with suggestions, a Maps link), Notes, and the Songs to rehearse, ticked from the show's setlist. The next one starts the day after the last, with the same times and place. Each rehearsal has Directions and a calendar button (your calendar app opens with it filled in); Add all to calendar puts every coming rehearsal into a calendar you choose at once (Melos asks for calendar access the first time), and adding again later updates them instead of doubling them. Tap a rehearsal to change it, long-press it to delete it. The Show tab shows the Next rehearsal.
  • Reminders: the evening before a show or a rehearsal, at 20:00, a notification reminds you of it (tap it to open the show). Melos asks to show notifications after you save your first show or rehearsal. Menu → Reminders… switches them off or changes the time. Cancelled shows don't remind you.
  • Shows and their rehearsals go along with sync and with Back up everything. When you share a setlist that has a show, Include the show adds its date, times, place and rehearsals, never the fee or your notes.

Live mode

Tap Go live on a setlist to play it on stage. The screen stays on.

  • Swipe sideways to move to the next or previous song: a short swipe (about a fifth of the screen) is enough, also on a score, and a quick flick works from anywhere.
  • Footswitch or page turner: pair a Bluetooth page turner (AirTurn, PageFlip, iRig BlueTurn, Donner and similar) in Android's Bluetooth settings; it works as a keyboard, no setup in Melos needed. In Live mode one pedal goes forward and the other back: on a score it turns the pages, on lyrics and notes it scrolls about a screen, and at the last page or the end of the text it goes to the next song (in a split view it works on the pane you touched last). Menu → Footswitch… switches to Songs only (every press changes song), turns on Volume keys too for turners that send volume up/down, swaps the pedals if forward and back come out the wrong way round, and shows each press so you can test them.
  • Setting up a footswitch: pair it in Android (Settings → Connected devices → Pair new device; it appears as a keyboard), then put the pedal in a mode that sends keys. Most page turners have several modes, chosen with a button combination or the maker's app (for example AirTurn Manager), often shown by the colour of a light; the pedal's manual lists them.
Pedal mode / what it sends In Melos
Page Down, right or down arrow, Space, Enter, media next Forward (next page, scroll down, then the next song)
Page Up, left or up arrow, media previous Back
Volume down / up ("camera" or "selfie" modes) Forward / back, with Volume keys too switched on
Mouse, touch, tap or swipe modes Not supported: they imitate a finger on the screen, not keys

Choose a mode called something like Page Up/Down, arrows, keyboard or one named for sheet-music readers, or media mode. To check, open Menu → Footswitch… and press each pedal: the line at the bottom shows what arrives ("Next ▸ (PAGE_DOWN)"). If nothing shows, the pedal is in a mouse or touch mode or not connected.

  • Choose what you see: Notes, Lyrics, Score or All (the split view). When the lyrics (or the notes) have a page as well as text, the page is shown; the Text button in the tools switches to the text (for transposing, the chords switch and text size) and Page switches back. The save button stores the choice with the song's layout, so the song always opens that way; without saving, the choice holds for the rest of the Live session for songs that have none saved. In the split view, pages share a pane with the score. The app remembers the view, text size and zoom for each song.
  • What the All view shows: in the song editor, Live mode view lists Notes, Lyrics and Score with a tick box each. Tick the ones you want in the split view and long-press a ticked row, then drag it, to set the order (first on top in portrait, first on the left in landscape). With one pane ticked the song opens on it alone; with nothing ticked it opens with the view you used last. With all three ticked, the notes and the lyrics share one pane and the score has its own. Panes a song has nothing for are skipped.
  • The divider between the two panes is slim, but you can grab it from a little way on either side of it.
  • Tools follow your finger: the row under the tabs shows only the tools of the pane you touched last (its name is shown at the left): rig cues, chords, Key and A−/A+ for notes and lyrics; invert, zoom and annotate for the score. The row scrolls sideways if it is still too wide.
  • Zoom for each page: with two pages on screen in All (say the score and a lyrics page), each keeps its own zoom: touch a page, then use the zoom buttons, and only that page grows or shrinks. The save button at the top (save this layout for the song) keeps each page's zoom with it.
  • A−/A+ change the text size of the part you last touched. In the shared notes-and-lyrics pane each part keeps the size it has on its own tab (the notes and the lyrics are sized separately), and Save stores them.
  • Chords in Live mode: next to the pedal button there is a piano button that shows or hides all chords (in the notes and the lyrics) for the whole session, and a Key button to move the chords up or down by semitones for this visit only (the key badge changes with them, the song itself is not changed, and it starts over with each visit).
  • Tempo pulse: the tempo pill in the header has a small ring that blinks on every beat, stronger and in the accent colour on the first beat of the bar (from the time signature). In 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 the tempo counts the groups of three eighths, so 6/8 blinks twice per bar. It is silent: there is no click. Tap the pill to switch the light off for this song (the ring stays, dimmed, like an unlit lamp) and tap it again to switch it back on. Each song keeps its own choice, so a ballad can stay dark while the up-tempo songs blink; new songs start with the light on. While off nothing runs, which also saves battery, and the blinking stops by itself when the app is in the background.
  • Tap the beat: the small bell beside the tempo pill works like the one in the song editor. Tap it a few times on the beat and the blinking follows your tempo; the pill turns into a tapped tempo with a small reset arrow (tapping the bell also lights it for this visit if the song has it off). This is only for now: the song's saved tempo never changes, and moving to another song or tapping the reset arrow brings the saved tempo back.
  • The bar at the right end of the tabs hides or shows the controls.
  • Leaving Live mode: tap the ✕ at the top right, or use the phone's Back gesture. Both end Live mode completely and the screen is then allowed to turn off again; Home only puts the app in the background, and Live mode is still there when you come back.
  • Where you are in the set: the counter at the top right ("3 / 8") is large and fills up like a progress bar behind the number as you move through the setlist.

Moving around

  • Swipe left or right on the Songs, Setlists and Shows screens to move between the tabs.
  • Inside a song or a setlist, swipe right to go back.
  • The knob button at the top opens the menu: import, share, back up, account and sync, Footswitch…, Language…, Help (this guide, available offline), theme (follow system, light, dark) and Colour themes….
  • Language: Melos speaks English, Greek, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Japanese and Korean. It follows the phone's language until you choose another: menu → Language… lists the nine, each written in its own language, and Same as the phone goes back. On Android 13 and newer this is the same setting as Android's Settings → Apps → Melos → Language. The Help guide is in English and Greek.
  • Colour themes (Melos Pro): menu → Colour themes… offers, besides the Melos colours, the light themes Ocean, Forest, Rose and Sepia and the dark themes Midnight, Plum and Stage (black and yellow, high contrast for dark stages). They colour the whole app, Live mode and this guide included. Choosing Follow system, Light or Dark in the menu goes back to the Melos colours. The same themes are in the Melos Companion (Help → Appearance). Without Pro the themes show with a lock; if Pro goes away, the app falls back to the Melos colours and keeps your choice for when it returns.

Sharing, import and backup

  • Share songs packs songs, or a whole setlist, into a .setlist file you can send to a band mate. You choose whether audio is included.
  • Import files (in the menu) opens .setlist files: pick one, or several at once and they are imported one after another, each with its own questions (Skip this file moves on to the next). You can also open a file from a message or Bluetooth. If a song already exists (same title and artist), you decide per song: Update takes the file's words, chords and details (key, tempo, rhythm, YouTube link) and keeps your files, score markings and Live settings, adding any files the song doesn't have yet (the ones it has are recognised and not added twice), which is the choice for songs edited in the Melos Companion; Keep both adds the imported song next to yours; Replace swaps everything on the song, files and markings included, for the imported one. If the file has a setlist with the same name and project as one of yours, you choose before importing: Update it (the default) gives your setlist the file's songs in the file's order, or Add it as a new setlist keeps yours and adds the imported one as "Name (2)".
  • Back up everything saves your whole library as one .setlist file. To restore it, open it with Import files.
  • These work with or without an online account.

Melos Companion: songs on a laptop or tablet

Typing songs on a phone is slow. The Melos Companion lets you prepare them on a bigger screen: open https://vakilos.github.io/melos-companion/ in Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox (free, nothing to sign up for). It speaks the same nine languages as the app (it follows the browser; Help → Appearance → Language for another).

  • Write lyrics and notes with the same chords in brackets, song sections and rig cues as in the app (rig cues in braces, {OD on}; typing Solo <OD on> writes it in braces too). Paste a chord sheet from the web, select it and use Make chords: chords written over the words move into them.
  • Attach PDFs, pictures, audio and text files, and build setlists.
  • Import a backup from the phone (Back up everything) to start from your library; Export makes a .setlist file to open with Melos on the phone. For songs already on the phone choose Update, so your files and score markings stay.
  • Install it from the browser (Chrome/Edge: the install icon in the address bar; Safari on Mac: File → Add to Dock; iPad/iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen) to use it offline in its own window. Desktop versions for Windows, macOS and Linux exist as well.
  • Everything stays in that browser on that computer; nothing is sent anywhere. Export everything now and then as a backup (the companion reminds you).

Sync across your devices (optional, Melos Pro)

With Melos Pro, sign in with your Google account and your songs and setlists follow you to your other phones and tablets. They are stored in a private app folder on your own Google Drive. Only Melos can see that folder; it does not show up in your normal Drive files and it counts against your Drive storage.

Turn it on

  1. Menu → Account and sync → Sign in with Google.
  2. Choose your account and allow access to the app's own Drive folder.
  3. Tap Sync now the first time. Do the same on your other devices.

What is synced

  • Always: songs, notes, lyrics, live layouts, setlists and projects.
  • Your choice: Audio files, and Scores (PDF and images) with the marks you drew on them.
  • Large files on Wi-Fi only (on by default) keeps audio and scores off mobile data. Text always syncs.

When it syncs

With Sync automatically on, Melos syncs shortly after you change something, every few hours, and when you open the app. If you are offline, your changes are kept on the phone and sent as soon as you are back online. Everything works offline; sync is only for sharing between devices.

Good to know

  • If you change the same song on two devices, the most recent change wins for that whole song.
  • If you delete a song or setlist, it is deleted on your other devices too. Keep a backup if unsure.
  • A song that exists on two devices with the same title and artist is treated as the same song, so it is not duplicated.
  • Turn audio or scores off and files already in Drive stay there; this device just stops fetching them. Turn them on again and they are downloaded on the next sync.
  • Sign out stops syncing on this phone. Your songs stay on the phone and in Drive.
  • To remove the synced copy from Drive: Google Account → Data & privacy → Third-party apps and services → Melos → delete its data.

If something fails

The Account and sync screen shows the result of the last sync. Check the internet connection, then tap Sync now. If it says Drive access needs your approval, sign in again.

Privacy

Everything is stored on your device. Purchases are handled by Google Play; Melos only learns whether you own Melos Pro. Only if you sign in for sync does Melos copy your songs, setlists, notes, annotations and the audio and score files you chose to your own Google Drive. Nothing is sent to any other server and nothing is shared with anyone.
